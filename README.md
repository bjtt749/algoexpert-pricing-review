# AlgoExpert review: what the $119 All Access bundle actually buys you, and when a lifetime plan makes more sense

If you're reading an AlgoExpert review, you're probably not browsing. You've got a loop coming up, a budget, and a nagging feeling that the $99 you read about somewhere is now $199 somewhere else. That feeling is correct, and it's the single most useful thing to sort out before you pay.

The company stopped selling per-product subscriptions on its public purchase page. Reviews published years apart quote three different price points, none of which match what the site shows today. So let's start with what AlgoExpert is, then what it costs right now, then the honest question: if you don't learn by watching someone talk through a problem on video, is there anything here worth $119?

## What AlgoExpert actually is in 2026

AlgoExpert is the interview prep platform Clément Mihailescu built after leaving Facebook. Its pitch has always been volume control: instead of 3,000+ problems on LeetCode and no idea where to start, you get a fixed list, an order to work through it, and a video attached to every question.

Here's the content inventory as the vendor describes it on the purchase page:

- **200 coding interview questions** across 15 categories — arrays, graphs, tries, linked lists, binary trees, strings, dynamic programming, searching, sorting, greedy, recursion, famous algorithms, heaps, BSTs, stacks — in four difficulty tiers
- Written solutions in **9 languages** (JavaScript, TypeScript, Python, Swift, Kotlin, C++, Java, C#, Go)
- **Two-part video explanations** per problem: the concept first, then the code — marketed as 100+ hours of video
- In-depth space-time complexity analysis for each question
- A **data structures crash course**, an in-browser IDE, **4 timed coding assessments**, and a peer mock-interview feature
- A certificate of completion, plus a referral claim for companies they partner with

One discrepancy worth flagging, because it shows up in nearly every review you'll read. Older write-ups consistently say **160 problems**. The vendor's own page says 200. Some reviews even say 100. If you're buying partly on count, check the live page rather than trusting a blog post, including this one.

SystemsExpert is the system design sibling, listed at **13 systems interview questions**. FrontendExpert covers 50 frontend questions plus HTML, CSS and JavaScript courses. MLExpert, InfraExpert, iOSExpert and BlockchainExpert round out the set. Behavioral prep appears on all of it as a single identical bullet: "bonus behavioral interview content." That word *bonus* is doing real work.

## The pricing problem: why every review disagrees

AlgoExpert's pricing has moved enough that comparisons across review sites are close to useless. Third-party checks published in **August 2026** found the purchase page showing a single card — **All Access, 1 year, $119, with $693 struck through** — under a flash-sale banner and a live countdown. A separate check the same day saw **$59 against a $343 list**, also with a timer running. In other words, the "price" is a sale price inside a moving window, and the honest answer to "how much does AlgoExpert cost" is: whatever the checkout page says when you're looking at it.

What the older numbers looked like:

| Tier (as older reviews describe it) | Price quoted | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AlgoExpert only | $99/year | Still quoted widely; not the SKU shown on the public purchase page in recent checks |
| AlgoExpert + SystemsExpert | $148/year | Same situation — legacy tier |

| All Access (7 products, 1 year) | $119 against a $693 list | What the public page showed in August 2026 checks, with a countdown attached |

A few things follow from that.

There's **no monthly option and no lifetime plan.** It's a fixed year. Renewal carries a roughly 30% loyalty discount according to multiple reviews, which brings a returning purchase down meaningfully — but you're buying again each cycle.

There's **no free trial** that gets you inside the platform, and the refund policy is described as strict to the point of being effectively final-sale. Several reviewers say it plainly: treat the purchase as non-refundable and decide before you click, not after.

And the **countdown is a countdown**. Whether it resets daily isn't something an outside observer can confirm, and one reviewer noted exactly that limitation. Don't let a timer make the decision for you. If the price has to be artificially urgent for you to buy, the tool probably isn't the bottleneck in your prep.

## Where AlgoExpert is genuinely good

The video explanations are the product, and by most accounts they're excellent for what they are. Two parts per problem — concept walkthrough, then implementation — with the instructor not skipping the middle steps. If you've ever stared at a written editorial and understood the code line by line while still having no idea how someone arrived at it, that format closes the gap. Reviews describe the teaching as patient and methodical rather than clever.

The curation is real too. Decision paralysis on LeetCode is a genuine failure mode, and a fixed 200-question list with a suggested order solves it. You open the app, continue where you left off, and don't spend twenty minutes choosing.

The workspace holds up. You write, run and test without leaving the page, and the language coverage is wider than most competitors at nine languages.

The **4 timed assessments** are the most underrated part and the least discussed. Sitting a coding round you can't pause, with a clock you didn't set, is much closer to an interview than untimed practice ever gets.

And in a market where nearly every competitor has rebuilt around an AI hint system, AlgoExpert's sell page lists no AI feature at all. Whether that's restraint or lag depends on how you study — but if you want an assistant that nudges instead of a video that explains, it's a real gap.

## Where it falls short

**No company-specific data.** AlgoExpert has no company tags, no frequency sorting, no "asked at Meta in the last six months" filtering. For the final two weeks before a named onsite, that matters more than anything else on this list, and it's the most common reason people end up buying LeetCode Premium on top.

**The list runs out.** Most reviewers land on the same conclusion: 200 curated questions is enough to learn patterns and not enough to build speed. Six to eight weeks of consistent work and you've finished it, at which point you're back on LeetCode anyway for volume. The purchase is a starting point, not the whole prep.

**Coverage gets thin at the edges.** Dynamic programming — heavily tested at top companies — is repeatedly called out as underweighted, along with advanced graph work, segment trees and monotonic stack patterns. Fine for a generalist loop, thin for a specialist one.

**System design is an upgrade, not a product.** SystemsExpert is a solid introduction and a poor substitute for real senior-level prep. If system design is a scored round for you, budget for something else.

**Behavioral prep is a bonus bullet.** Amazon's leadership principles, Meta's behavioral screen, Google's Googleyness — none of that is covered at any tier.

Add the strict refund policy and the missing free trial, and you're making a $119 decision with no hands-on preview and no recourse. Sample videos on YouTube are the closest thing to a test drive.

## Who should buy it, and who shouldn't

Buy it if you learn well from video, you're within roughly two months of interviews, you want a fixed list with an endpoint, and you'd rather pay once for a year of structured content than manage a monthly subscription.

Skip it if you already read faster than you watch, if you need company-tagged problems for a specific onsite, if system design and behavioral rounds are on your loop, or if you've already ground through 300+ LeetCode problems and mostly need reps rather than teaching.

The budget version, for the record: free lists and free pattern guides cover a surprising amount of what people were paying for in 2021. The thing you're actually buying is production quality and an order to follow.

## The alternative to look at first if you're a reader, not a watcher

The biggest structural complaint about AlgoExpert is the format: everything is delivered as video, so re-checking one detail costs you four minutes of scrubbing instead of forty seconds of scanning. If that's your problem, **AlgoMonster** is worth a serious look before you spend anything.

The teaching unit is the pattern, not the problem. You learn why two pointers applies, get the generalised implementation, then work variations of it until the identification step becomes automatic.

The parts that hold up in third-party reviews:

Two caveats, stated plainly because they matter. There is **no video**, so if you've learned everything you know by watching people code, this will feel like a different sport. And system design coverage is introductory — the same limit AlgoExpert's SystemsExpert has, at the senior end. Several third-party write-ups also mention a job-offer guarantee; the terms behind that aren't something you can assess from outside, so read the fine print rather than the headline.

## Common questions

### Is AlgoExpert worth it?

For a specific reader, yes: someone interviewing in four to eight weeks who learns from video and wants a fixed, finishable list with an in-browser workspace. The 4 timed assessments and the curated order are the parts you can't easily replicate free. For someone who already writes code fluently and just needs volume and company tags, it's an expensive detour.

### Does AlgoExpert cover system design?

Not in the base product. System design lives in SystemsExpert, which is one of the seven products in the All Access bundle. It's 13 questions — a solid vocabulary builder, not enough for an L5+ design round at a top company.

### Can I get a refund?

Multiple reviews describe AlgoExpert's refund policy as strict, with no free trial to test the platform first. Plan the purchase as final. AlgoMonster's free tier is the lower-risk way to find out whether text-based learning suits you before paying for anything.

### Should I use both?

Plenty of people do, and the split most reviewers recommend is pattern learning first, volume practice second. Learn the framework on one platform, then take the reps elsewhere. What you shouldn't do is buy three subscriptions in the same month and use none of them properly — that's a $400 mistake a lot of candidates make.

## The bottom line

AlgoExpert is a well-produced video course with a clear endpoint and a workspace that works. It's also annual-only, currently sold as one bundle inside a countdown, unrehearsed on refunds, and thin on company-specific data and behavioral prep. The $119 you may see and the $199 other reviews quote describe different eras of the same product, and neither figure is worth trusting over the checkout page.

If you want to watch someone reason through a problem and don't mind paying a premium for polish, it holds up. If you'd rather read a pattern, get a template, and drill it until you stop hesitating at the whiteboard — and you'd like to pay once instead of annually — the reader-first option is the better use of the same money.
